# DARIA.EXE validation plan

The current release proves that the game loop works. The next question is whether people voluntarily engage with it as a game rather than merely understanding the interface.

## What to observe

- case start → final decision completion;
- number of clues/actions used before deciding;
- early-decision vs full-investigation behavior;
- replay / second-case behavior;
- return play after the first session;
- share / invite behavior;
- whether players report that the outcome felt fair rather than arbitrary;
- whether players can identify what changed their mind after the debrief.

## Design guardrails

- no pre-choice “correctness” hints;
- no reward for guessing the designer's intended answer;
- evidence should change the state, not just reveal trivia;
- debrief may explain consequences, but should not rewrite the player's path as obviously wrong in hindsight.

## Stronger evidence later

A learning or reasoning-improvement claim would require a separate study design. Completion, replay and qualitative engagement are product evidence; they are **not** evidence that the game improves cognition.

## Current boundary

Browser validation covers the mechanics across desktop and mobile profiles. Retention, replay, learning effect and monetisation remain unproven.
